## The parse

`crates/arreo-core/src/state/engine.rs`: `scan_bell(bytes, &mut osc_active, &mut esc_pending)`
replaces `if bytes.contains(&0x07)` in `feed`. State machine:

- outside an OSC string: `ESC` → `esc_pending` (the pair is consumed on the next byte),
  `0x07` → **bell**;
- `ESC ]` → inside an OSC string;
- inside: `0x07` (BEL), `0x9c` (C1 ST), `ESC \` (ST) → terminator, **not** a bell;
  `0x18`/`0x1a` (CAN/SUB) → abort, string over;
- everything else → unchanged.

`osc_active`/`esc_pending` live on the `Engine`, so an OSC string, its `ESC ]` opener, or the
`ESC` of an ST can straddle two `feed` calls (the daemon polls arbitrary chunks).

Re-packing: the two TUI fixtures are the recorded raw bytes re-windowed into `.pty` events
(768 B per 10 s capture) because the original PTY transcript was captured with `script(1)`,
not `arreo record` — the byte stream, which is the whole point of the fixture, is the recorded
one byte for byte, and the argv header names the command that produced it. `bell-bare.pty`
and `osc-terminator.pty` are synthetic on purpose (they encode the two halves of the rule; no
harness in the captures emits a bare BEL), which is exactly what the acceptance criteria ask
for. Every recorded fixture used an isolated `HOME`/`$PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR` and its own
`--session-dir` under `target/test-scratch/T-0080`; `scan_secrets` is clean on all eight.
